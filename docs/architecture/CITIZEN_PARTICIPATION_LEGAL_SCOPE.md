# JanaVani Citizen-Participation Legal Scope

## Product boundary

JanaVani is a citizen-participation and civic-action preparation system. It is
not the separate future Legal Companion product.

This version may use legal knowledge when it materially helps a citizen's
civic action, including drafting a complaint, representation, RTI, objection,
follow-up, escalation or evidence-related document. It must not overwhelm the
citizen with unrelated legal domains.

## Court judgments as optional drafting support

For a matter where judicial reasoning would materially strengthen a civic
document, JanaVani may search for relevant Supreme Court and jurisdictionally
relevant High Court judgments and include verified citations/references in the
draft. This is optional and subject-matter dependent; most routine complaints
do not need case-law citations.

Search priority:

1. Supreme Court judgments where directly relevant.
2. The relevant High Court, especially where the matter is jurisdiction-specific.
3. Other verified judicial material only when it materially assists the matter.

A judgment reference must be verified against an authoritative/approved court
source and should record the case title, date, citation/neutral citation where
available, court, legal issue, and the proposition for which it is being cited.

Do not cite a judgment merely because its keywords look similar. The system
must distinguish factual similarity from the legal proposition actually decided.

Do not manufacture, paraphrase as a holding, or guess a citation. If a judgment
cannot be verified, it must not appear as a verified authority in the final
document.

The Supreme Court provides official judgment search facilities and notes that
original court records are authoritative. Its public landmark-judgment
summaries are useful for understanding decisions but expressly are not part of
the Court's decision/reasons and are not for use in legal proceedings. Therefore
JanaVani should prefer the actual judgment over a summary.

The official High Court/eCourts service supports searches by party, case number,
act, case type and order/judgment date and provides judgment/order documents.

## AI safety rule for case law

AI-generated citations must never be trusted without source verification. A
recent Supreme Court judgment, *Pooja Ramesh Singh v. Jammu and Kashmir Bank
Ltd.*, 2026 INSC 668, addressed the problem of AI-generated fake or hallucinated
case citations. This is a direct reason to make citation verification a hard
system boundary, not a prompt instruction.

## Scope exclusion

The following are reserved for a future, separate Legal Companion product and
are not part of this citizen-participation version as a general-purpose legal
service:

- exhaustive legal research across every field;
- litigation strategy as a standalone service;
- legal representation;
- individualized legal-opinion practice;
- comprehensive contract-law advisory;
- specialist corporate, tax, securities, insolvency, IP or professional-law
  practice workflows unless a narrow civic-participation use case requires a
  verified reference.

The current legal knowledge catalog may contain these domains as future
reference metadata, but they should not automatically activate in the citizen
workflow.

## Document boundary

When legal sources or judgments are used, they strengthen the citizen's draft;
they do not change JanaVani's delivery boundary. JanaVani prepares the final
letter/complaint/RTI/representation/etc. and delivers it as the citizen-selected
PDF or DOCX. What the citizen does after receiving the file is outside JanaVani.
