# 1. PURPOSE

This document defines the long-term direction of Janavani.

It is not a technical architecture document.

It is not a current implementation checklist.

It is not a commitment to build every capability described here.

It defines the destination toward which Janavani may evolve.

The purpose is to ensure that individual features, technologies, interfaces, and future experiments remain aligned with one central objective:

> **Strengthen the citizen's ability to understand, participate in, engage with, and hold public institutions accountable through lawful, evidence-based, privacy-preserving technology.**

5. CONSTITUTIONAL FOUNDATION

Janavani's long-term civic orientation is grounded in the constitutional framework of India.

The Constitution, fundamental rights, statutory rights, lawful procedures, democratic participation, and accountable public administration should remain central reference points.

Janavani must not present itself as a substitute for courts, lawyers, constitutional authorities, statutory authorities, or elected institutions.

Its purpose is to improve citizen understanding and lawful participation.

6. ARTICLES 14, 19 AND 21

The relationship between Articles 14, 19 and 21 is an important part of Indian constitutional jurisprudence.

The Supreme Court's decision in:

Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India (1978)

is associated with the development of the understanding that these fundamental rights are interconnected rather than isolated compartments.

The relevant constitutional provisions are:

Article 14

Equality before law and equal protection of the laws.

Article 19

Protection of specified freedoms, subject to constitutionally permitted restrictions.

Article 21

Protection of life and personal liberty.

Janavani may use this jurisprudence as one constitutional reference framework when helping citizens understand public measures, government actions, or proposed representations.

It must clearly distinguish:

Constitutional text
        â†“
Judicial interpretation
        â†“
Statutory law
        â†“
Administrative action
        â†“
Janavani explanation

Janavani must not present its own interpretation as binding law.
